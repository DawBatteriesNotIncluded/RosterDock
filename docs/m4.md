# Milestone 4 notes: Peter

What landed, how Peter works, what the done criterion means in the tree, and what is deliberately left for
later. The plan card: Peter as a loop that takes a goal, decomposes it with done definitions, assigns by role
and load, watches runs and reviews, re-plans on failure and escalates only at gates, on events and on a
schedule; a research path with document artifacts and a checklist review; department leads as scoped PM
instances; recursion and cost caps per goal with a spend report; Peter as company architect proposing
departments, teammates, toolboxes and policies as drafts, and auditing an existing company. Done when a run
failure is re-planned without a human noticing.

## How a goal moves

1. `POST /goals` with the text (and optional department and caps). Peter is seated if the company somehow
   has no project manager, a project opens named after the goal, and Peter plans before the request returns.
2. Planning: with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` on the board Peter asks his model (the model on his teammate row) for
   one structured plan, with the roster (handles, roles, load, departments) in the system prompt. The board
   validates it: every assignee exists and is neither the manager nor a reviewer, dependencies point at
   earlier tasks only, outputs are flat file names, the task cap holds. A plan that fails, a refusal or a
   failed call falls back to the heuristic and `goals.planner` says why. Without a key the heuristic plans:
   research (a researcher), requirements (a requirements teammate), build (an engineer), each waiting on the
   previous, assigned to the least loaded teammate of that role, in the routed department first.
3. Questions: the model may ask up to four once. The goal is `needs_you` with the questions;
   `POST /goals/:id/answers` records the answers and Peter plans again, told he may not ask twice. The
   heuristic never asks; with nobody on the roster to do the work it escalates (`goal.escalated`, attention
   text) instead.
4. Handoff: tasks are created by Peter (`created_by = teammate`), with the goal's department (or the model's
   pick, or the assignee's), a lifetime budget lands on the project when the goal has a token cap, the
   project takes the plan's title and done definition, `goal.planned` names the tasks and assignees, and
   every task with no dependency starts at once.
5. Spend: `GET /goals/:id` returns `spend`: tokens the runs used on the goal's project (with cost from the
   price table), Peter's own planning tokens and calls, the total, and the cap.

## Department leads

Department leads are Peter instances scoped to their department. `managerFor(department)` is the lead when
the department has one and the company Peter otherwise; every step of the loop, the ready-task starter and
the chaser resolve the manager per task, so a lead only ever acts on its own department's tasks and in its
own name (the events say Archy planned, Archy re-planned, Archy chased). The company Peter routes: a goal
handed in with a department is planned by that department's lead from its members; a goal without one is
planned by Peter, and when every assignee sits in one department the goal is routed there at handoff and
the lead tends it from then on. A lead can also be assigned work; then Peter manages that one task, because
nobody manages their own (`managerForTask`). The lead's model, roster reads and caps are the same code; the
scope is the department.

## The research path

A task is `build` (the default: its diff is reviewed) or `research` (`workKind`, set by Peter's plan for
researchers and requirements teammates, or by hand). A research task's pack has a "Research" section: the
deliverable is documents, every document ends with a Sources section. The worker harvests documents as it
always did (a synthetic new-file diff when there is no repository), so anchored comments still work. At
review time a research task gets the `sources` check alongside secrets, outputs and evaluation: every
document artifact must have a Sources or References heading with at least one entry, or links in the text.
The reviewer's pack says "This is a research review" and lists the checklist (findings answer the brief,
claims cite sources, options compared, open questions listed, no credential values); the decision tools are
the same. The stub engine writes a Sources line into documents when the pack asks for research, so the
demo path stays green without a model.

## Peter as architect

`POST /drafts/org` with a brief ("I need a finance function that reconciles Xero weekly") records a draft
of kind `org`: a summary and departments, each with a write policy, a one-sentence policy, a toolbox (MCP
server names the department needs: a recommendation, granted by nobody until M5's approvals) and
teammates (a global template by name, or a fresh persona; one lead per department). With a model, Peter
writes it against the templates and the org as it stands, and the board validates it (no existing
department, no taken name, templates that exist); otherwise the heuristic maps the brief's words to the
same department templates the web's "Add a department" offers (Finance, Research, Marketing ops,
Engineering, Docs), skips departments that exist, and numbers taken names. Nothing exists until an owner
or admin accepts: `POST /drafts/:id/accept` re-validates and creates the teammates (persona, engine and
model from the template; a disabled engine falls back to Claude Code) and the departments with their lead
and members in one mutation, and records the outcome on the draft. A draft that no longer fits is refused
with the reason. `POST /drafts/audit` records a draft of kind `audit`: idle teammates (no open task, no
run in 14 days), teammates in no department, departments without a lead or without a reviewer while
someone does work, read-only departments whose sandbox allows a shell or the network, and a company with
teammates but no departments. Accepting an audit acknowledges it and creates nothing.

## How Peter watches

A pass (`peterPass`) is the whole loop for one company, safe to repeat and safe to run from two replicas at
once. It runs every `ROSTERDOCK_PETER_INTERVAL_MS` (30 s) for every company with a project manager, and about
300 ms after any of these events land for a company: `goal.created`, `goal.answered`, `run.failed`,
`run.blocked`, `run.completed`, `run.stopped`, `task.done`, `task.blocked`, `review.changes_requested`,
`review.approved`, `review.vetoed`, `pr.failed`, `budget.alert`. Passes for one company never overlap; an
event during a pass queues one more. In tests the loop is off and passes run by hand or through the route.

1. **Goals still planning** (a board restarted mid-plan) are planned.
2. **Failed runs.** A teammate task blocked by its run (any blocker that is not a dependency, a teammate or an
   approval) with no live run is re-planned: Peter writes a note (the model reads the error, summary and
   handover and writes one to three sentences; the heuristic names the error and says start from what
   exists, do less, set a blocker instead of failing), `tasks.replans` goes up, `task.replanned` lands, the
   note is posted on the task, and the assignee is started again with the note in the pack. After
   `MAX_REPLANS` (2) the third failure escalates: the task stays blocked and the goal turns amber
   (`needs_you`, `goal.escalated`) with the last error. The model can escalate earlier by answering
   `needsPerson` (a missing credential, an unreachable system); so does the heuristic when the error looks like
   one. A task outside any goal gets a "Needs a person" message instead, once per six hours.
3. **Reviews sent back** whose author could not be restarted at decision time (engine off, budget) are
   restarted on the next pass. A review at `MAX_REVIEW_ROUNDS` (3) with changes requested escalates the goal.
   A review waiting for a person (no reviewer teammate) is a gate and is left alone.
4. **Load.** A teammate with more than 3 open tasks hands their newest unstarted Peter-created task to a
   colleague of the same role with at least two fewer (`task.assigned` with the reason).
5. **Ready tasks** start: teammate-created (Peter's or a teammate's), assigned to a teammate, every
   dependency Done, no live run, the goal neither stopped nor waiting on a person. Hand-made tasks are never
   started by Peter: a person may be drafting.
6. **Chasing** as in M2, now department-aware.
7. **Goals whose every task is Done** close with `goal.done` carrying the spend.

The done criterion: `loop.test.ts` fails a run through the worker protocol, calls one pass, and finds the
assignee running again with Peter's note in its pack and nobody having touched the board; the third failure
turns the goal amber and a further pass changes nothing.

## Demo script

Offline (the tests are the demo path): `pnpm test`. `goals.test.ts` hands Peter a goal by heuristic and by
recorded model responses (questions, answers, a refused plan, a refusal); `loop.test.ts` fails a run through
the worker protocol and watches one pass re-plan it with nobody touching the board, then escalate on the
third failure, start dependants when their dependency is Done, restart a sent-back author, rebalance load,
close a goal with its spend, and wake on events; `leads.test.ts` has Archy plan, start, re-plan and chase
Engineering's goal; `reviews.test.ts` reviews a research task against the checklist; `architect.test.ts`
proposes and accepts a finance function and audits a company.

Live, with the dev stack:

```bash
pnpm db:migrate && pnpm dev            # board + web; Peter's loop runs every 30 s
pnpm dev:worker                        # only when you want real runs: about 200k tokens each
```

1. Company page, **New goal**: "Send a weekly digest of Gong calls with next steps to #sales every
   Friday." Peter plans before the modal returns: with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` on the board he may ask up to
   four questions (answer them in the modal or on the goal card); without it the heuristic plans research,
   requirements and build by role. The modal lists the tasks and who does what; the goal card shows Live,
   progress, assignees and spend; the feed says "Peter split … into 3 tasks for Ricky, Brody, Cody".
2. With a worker running, watch the research task finish, its review open with the sources check and
   the reviewer's checklist, the requirements task start on its own when the research is Done, and so on
   to the build's pull request. Kill a run mid-way (or use a stub teammate with `[stub:fail]` in a
   brief): the task blocks, and within a pass Peter re-plans it with a note in the next pack and a
   message on the task. Do it three times and the goal turns amber with the reason.
3. **Design from a brief**: "I need a finance function that reconciles Xero weekly." A draft appears
   under Peter proposes: Finance, every write asks, Finn as lead and a Verifier, toolbox xero as a
   recommendation. Accept and create: the department and teammates exist; the toolbox is granted by nobody.
4. **Audit the company**: findings for idle and unseated teammates, departments without a reviewer or a
   lead, and sandboxes that reach a shell from a read-only department. Noted or Not now.

### What the demo showed

Run in this chat against the dev company GTM Studio with no model key and no worker: the goal opened
project "Send a weekly digest…", Peter split it into #10 (research, Ricky) and #11 (build, Stubby, a stub
teammate whose role says engineer), started #10, and the card, modal and feed read as designed. The loop,
running in the dev board on its timer, had already found #3 ("E2E: worker killed mid-run", blocked since
M1) and re-planned it twice on its own before anyone opened the page, which is the done criterion seen in
the wild. The audit found four things (three unseated teammates, no reviewer in Engineering); the finance
draft proposed Finn and a Verifier 2 (a Verifier exists). Both were declined to leave the company as it was.

## Not in M4 (by design)

- The Claude Agent SDK loop for Peter (see the decisions); toolbox grants (M5, the drafts carry the names);
  goal caps and the token cap edited from the UI after creation; money on the goal card (tokens only; the
  API has the cost); Peter stopping queued runs when a goal is dropped; a research reviewer that fetches
  the sources it checks; the MCP route accepting teammate tokens with a manager scope.
- All but the first shipped after M4. The Agent SDK loop stays dropped: Peter's model calls are single
  structured calls (below).

## Decisions taken while building

- Peter's model calls are single structured calls, not an agent loop. A plan, a re-plan note or an org
  proposal is one JSON document, and a loop that must run offline, cheaply and on every event is
  deterministic board code. The Claude Code loop stays in the worker for teammates; Peter plans with the
  Messages API through the SDK, with `fetch` injected so tests replay recorded response shapes (written
  from the API documentation, as the GitHub fixtures were). The key is `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` on the board, by
  name; nothing else about the model is stored.
- Every decision Peter makes has a heuristic. A company with no key still plans, watches and re-plans; the
  model makes the plan better, never possible. Tests run both paths.
- Caps live on the goal and are enforced where tasks are created, for every teammate including Peter, so a
  runaway delegation chain (M2's lesson) stops at the cap without a person watching.
- Escalation is one thing: the goal turns amber with a sentence saying why. Peter never pages anyone and
  never touches a gate: merging and live writes stay with people, and a review waiting for a person waits.
- The loop is a pass, not a state machine. Every step derives what to do from the board as it is, so a
  missed event costs one interval, a crash mid-pass costs nothing, and two replicas can both run it.
